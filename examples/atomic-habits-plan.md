# Atomic Habits — action plan

**90-day outcome:** Asleep by 10:30pm on school nights. Measured by nights the
phone charges in the kitchen, logged on the fridge calendar — not by how
rested I feel, which I'm apparently a terrible judge of.

**Week shape and constraints:** High school chemistry teacher. Up at 5:45am,
school 7:30–3:30, grading most evenings 7–9pm. Two teenagers, so the house is
loud until nine. The 9–11pm window is the only quiet time I get, which is
exactly the problem.

**Already tried:** Screen-time limits (dismissed them within a week), a
"no phone after 9" rule (lasted four days), and moving the charger to the
hallway (carried the phone to bed anyway). All three broke at the same
moment: 9pm arrives, I'm tired, and the phone is already in my hand.

**Accountability:** Weekly, Sunday night.

**My system, not my goal:** Phone goes on the kitchen charger when I sit down
to grade, not when I go to bed. Set in session on 2026-09-10 (ch 1).

**Identity:** "I'm the kind of person who protects the last hour of her day."
Set in session on 2026-09-13 (ch 2).

## Ch 1 — The Surprising Power of Atomic Habits

**Status:** done
**The claim:** Habits compound, so the results of any single day are too small
to notice and the outcomes of a good system are always delayed — which is why
goals are the wrong unit. You do not rise to the level of your goals; you fall
to the level of your systems.
**Why it matters here:** Three previous attempts were all goals ("no phone
after 9") with no system attached. Nothing specified what would physically
happen instead, so at 9pm there was nothing to fall back to.
**Author's challenge (in-field):** None prescribed — chapter 1 is the
argument for systems, not an exercise. The in-field step below is derived
from it, and the reader was told so.
**Author's challenge (in-session, done 2026-09-10):** Restate the goal as a
system. → Phone on the kitchen charger at grading time, recorded above.

**What they said:** "Every version of this I've tried was a rule about what
not to do at 9pm. The charger thing is the first one that's about 7pm, when
I still have some willpower left." On why the hallway charger failed: "there
was no moment attached to it. It was just a place."

**Commitment:** Wed 2026-09-11, when you sit down to grade at 7pm, put the
phone on the kitchen charger first. Once, one night, logged on the fridge
calendar. First move is setting the phone down before opening the laptop.
**Review on:** 2026-09-12
**Takeaway:** Attaching it to grading instead of to bedtime was the entire
difference, and it took ninety seconds to set up. Three years of failed
attempts were all scheduled at the moment I had the least willpower. The
system doesn't need to be strong if it runs early.

**Parked for later:**
- The 1 percent framing — find something else to apply it to once this holds.

## Ch 2 — How Your Habits Shape Your Identity (and Vice Versa)

**Status:** dropped
**The claim:** Behaviour that conflicts with self-image does not last, so the
durable path runs through identity rather than outcomes. Clear's prescribed
route is a two-step process: decide the type of person you want to be, then
prove it to yourself with small wins — every action being a vote for that
person.
**Why it matters here:** Step one landed hard and is in the header. Step two
did not, and the reason is recorded below rather than smoothed over.
**Author's challenge (in-field):** Prove the new identity with a small win —
cast one visible vote for it.
**Author's challenge (in-session, done 2026-09-13):** Decide the type of
person you want to be, working backwards from the outcome. Asked "who is the
type of person who is asleep by 10:30 on a school night?" → identity
recorded in the header.

**What they said:** "Deciding who I want to be was genuinely useful and took
four minutes. But the small-wins part, the way I was going to do it — telling
my husband 'I'm someone who protects her evenings' — that's an affirmation,
and I'd feel like a fraud saying it before it's true. I'd rather the evidence
come first."

**Commitment:** Tell one person the identity sentence out loud before Sun
2026-09-14, as a first vote for it.
**Review on:** 2026-09-14
**Takeaway:** The identity question works; announcing it doesn't, for me. I
don't get conviction from saying a thing and then living up to it, I get it
from a streak on the fridge calendar that's hard to argue with. Clear says
every action is a vote — fine, but I want to count the votes, not campaign.

**Parked for later:**

## Ch 3 — How to Build Better Habits in 4 Simple Steps

**Status:** committed
**The claim:** Every habit runs the same four-step loop — cue, craving,
response, reward — and habits exist to solve a recurring problem with as
little effort as possible. The Four Laws of Behaviour Change each attack one
step, so you have to know which step your habit turns on before you know
which law to apply.
**Why it matters here:** The charger system is working on the 7pm cue, but
nobody has established what the actual 9pm cue is. Three previous attempts
all guessed at it. The reward is also unidentified, and the chapter's claim
is that the habit is solving a real problem — so it's worth knowing which.
**Author's challenge (in-field):** None prescribed here; the Habits Scorecard
arrives in ch 4. The observation below is derived from the four-step model,
and the reader was told the exercise proper is one chapter away.
**Author's challenge (in-session, done 2026-09-16):** Break the existing
habit into the four steps from memory. → Cue: "not sure, that's the point."
Craving: "to not be a teacher for a bit." Response: scroll. Reward: "twenty
minutes where nobody needs anything from me."

**What they said:** "I can name the reward instantly and I have no idea what
the cue is. That's uncomfortable." On the craving: "it's not the phone. It's
that grading ends and there's no version of the evening that's mine."

**Commitment:** Wed 2026-09-16 through Fri 2026-09-18, the moment you reach
for the phone after grading, write down on the fridge calendar what happened
in the ten seconds before — where you were, what you'd just finished, what
you were feeling. Three nights, one line each. No changing the behaviour this
week, only recording it. First move is putting the pen on top of the calendar
now.
**Review on:** 2026-09-19
